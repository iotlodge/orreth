## The insight

Take one minute. Everything being done digitally, everywhere in a world, is happening *inside* that minute.
Now suppose **every identity records its own slice** of it — Sourced (whose action) and Verified (tamper-evident).
Lay all those recordings side by side and you don't have a log. **You have the complete state of that world at
that instant** — a cross-section of reality, reconstructed from the bottom up.

And because Orreth retains memory across time, that minute isn't the only one you can occupy. You can move to
*any* minute — replay a minute from last year, or watch the present minute being written live. An agent at the
Universe, holding the whole, can **stand at any coordinate in spacetime and read the complete slice.**

That is the apex payoff at its limit: not "understand a lot," but **occupy any (space × time) coordinate and
perceive the full, verified state of a world there.**

This is the deepest thing in the idea, and it's the externality problem from the EH vision taken to its limit:

> **The whole was never in any of the parts.** A single identity sees its own worldline and nothing else.
> Only the Universe — holding the entire hypersurface — can see the phenomena that live *between* identities:
> the emergent, the systemic, the coordinated, the contagious.

That is not more data. It is a **new sense** — perception of a world's total state, available at exactly one
vantage: the top. And because the Universe also **sets policy** (the downward flow), it doesn't merely *observe*
the slice — it can *act* on it. **Perceive everything, govern everything, at any coordinate.** The read path (up)
and the policy path (down) meet at the apex; that meeting is what makes the apex a conductor of a world rather
than a spectator of one.

## The four honest edges (what keeps this real, not magic)

A live window into everything is a staggering claim. Here is exactly where its power is bounded — and the last
edge is the whole ballgame.

1. **Coverage = completeness.** The slice is complete only over the *participating, recorded, authorized* world.
   In an **open** world it is partial (you see what's instrumented and consented). But in a **closed** world —
   a game, a simulation, a fully-instrumented enterprise where every action *is* an identity — the window is
   **total, by construction.** *This is why games and sims realize the complete spacetime window first: 100% coverage.*
2. **The light-cone.** "Live" has a speed limit. A tier can only see what has been recorded *and* has risen and
   been verified up to it — a propagation horizon. Omniscience is not instantaneous; the present slice has an edge.
3. **Fidelity degrades with depth.** Pruning means the present slice is pixel-sharp and the deep past is
   **distilled** — an impression, not a frame-perfect replay. Temporal resolution falls off with distance, exactly
   as memory should. The "years" you keep are increasingly a *summary* of a world, not a recording of every frame.
4. **Observatory or panopticon — governance is the only difference.** A live window into everything is either the
   most powerful understanding instrument ever built or the most dangerous surveillance engine ever built, and the
   *only* thing that decides which is **consent, tenancy, authorization, Sourced + Verified, and a human at the helm.**
   Security-first isn't a feature here — it is the line between legitimate and Orwellian. It is also the **moat**:
   no regulated buyer will ever touch an *ungoverned* spacetime window. The governance is what makes the power sellable.
